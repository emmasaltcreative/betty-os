## 2. Recommended Creative Concept

**"The Hour Before Anyone Arrives"**

The apartment is mid-afternoon, unhurried. Someone has been reading. The candle is already burning — not placed for a photo. The book cover is worn into the scene, not centered in it. We're documenting a mood that already exists, not constructing one.

This concept works specifically because:
- Soft afternoon light does the heavy lifting — no setup required
- An NYC apartment at this hour has natural warmth and shadow that reads as lived-in, not styled
- The restraint of two products keeps the session focused and prevents over-propping

## 4. Shot List in Production Order

Work with the light as it moves. Shoot toward the window first while it's brightest; close-ups last as light softens.

---

### Block 1 — Establish the Scene (First 30 min)
*Window or windowsill zone. Candle burning. Natural light at its peak.*

**Shot 1 — Candle, Ambient Hero**
- Candle positioned near window, slightly off-center in frame
- Let light hit the wax, not just the flame
- Vertical. Negative space above or to one side for text overlay
- Capture with flame; capture without flame as backup

**Shot 2 — Wide Lifestyle Pull**
- Step back. Show the corner: candle + a book with the book cover on it, resting naturally on a surface (chair arm, side table, windowsill)
- No fussing. If something in frame feels wrong, remove it entirely rather than rearranging
- Horizontal for waitlist use

**Shot 3 — Both Products, Contextual Vignette**
- Candle and covered book sharing the frame, neither competing for center
- Natural offset composition — use the rule of thirds loosely
- Vertical orientation

---

### Block 2 — Book Cover Featured (Next 30 min)
*Move slightly away from window. Softer, more directional light.*

**Shot 4 — Book Cover on Book, Surface Rest**
- Book closed or open with cover visible, resting on a surface with slight texture (wood, linen, marble)
- Slight camera angle — not flat lay, not fully side-on. Approximately 30–40° overhead
- One hand holding or touching the book is acceptable if it feels natural; do not force it

**Shot 5 — Book Cover Texture / Edge Close-Up**
- Fill the frame with material detail
- Look for stitching, grain, closure hardware, or the spine edge
- Shoot in portrait. Keep focus shallow

---

### Block 3 — Candle Detail + Selects (Final 30 min)
*Light is warmest now. Best for intimate close-ups.*

**Shot 6 — Candle Flame + Wax Close-Up**
- Get close. Show the melt pool, the texture of the wax, the way flame light falls
- Square crop in mind, but shoot vertical and crop in post
- Avoid centering the flame — let it sit in the upper third

**Shot 7 (if time allows) — Found Detail**
- Something incidental: the candle's label at an angle, the book cover's shadow on a wall, a hand mid-page-turn
- Shoot instinctively. If it doesn't happen naturally, skip it

## 5. Styling and Prop Notes

**Keep it spare. Every object in frame should earn its place.**

**Surfaces to use (in order of preference):**
- Windowsill with natural patina
- Wood side table or desk with some wear
- Upholstered chair arm (linen, bouclé, or aged fabric preferred)
- Avoid: glossy surfaces, white IKEA shelving, anything visually generic

**Props allowed:**
- The book the cover is on — choose a real book you'd actually read (spine should be visible and feel authentic; a worn paperback is better than a pristine hardcover dressed for a shoot)
- One additional book, stacked beneath or nearby — not for emphasis, just for believability
- A glass of water, tea, or wine *only* if it's already there and looks right

**Props to remove from frame:**
- Chargers, cords, remotes
- Anything with visible logos unrelated to the brand
- Decorative objects that feel deliberately curated

**What to wear (if hands appear):**
- No polish, or a quiet neutral
- A simple ring is fine; avoid anything that draws the eye

## 6. Lighting and Composition Direction

**Light:**
- Soft afternoon light through a north- or west-facing window is ideal for this hour
- Do not diffuse with a white sheet or bounce board — let the natural falloff happen
- If light is too harsh and direct, move 2–3 feet back from the window or angle the subject 45° away
- Avoid overhead room lighting; turn off ceiling lights and lamps unless they add warmth at a very low level

**Exposure:**
- Slight underexposure (−0.3 to −0.7 EV) preserves warmth and avoids the overlit look that reads as staged
- Shoot RAW if possible

**Composition:**
- Favor asymmetry over centering
- Let negative space exist — it reads as editorial, not empty
- For Pinterest: shoot vertical (4:5 or 2:3 ratio) natively; do not crop verticals from horizontal frames
- Avoid dead-center compositions except for intentional close-up texture shots

**What "unstaged" actually means in practice:**
- If you find yourself adjusting something for the third time, stop and shoot it as-is
- Imperfection in surface, position, or lighting is usually an asset
- A candle that's been burning for 20 minutes looks better than one just lit

## 7. Platform-Specific Adaptations

### Pinterest
- **Format:** 2:3 vertical primary (1000 × 1500 px); some 1:1 squares for texture shots
- **Aesthetic register:** These should function as aspirational saves — images someone pins to a board called "reading nook" or "slow living" and forgets they pinned until they see your product again
- **Text overlay:** Minimal or none on the image itself; let the Pin description carry the copy weight
- **Board strategy:** Consider a dedicated *Oh Betty Jaletti — The Reading Hour* board; also seed into broader lifestyle boards (reading, candles, home)
- **Key images for Pinterest:** Shot 1 (candle hero), Shot 2 (wide lifestyle), Shot 5 (book cover texture)

### Waitlist
- **Format:** Hero image should be horizontal (Shot 2) or a tall vertical with enough negative space for text overlay
- **Tone:** The waitlist page image should make someone feel like they've already arrived at the experience — not like they're looking at a product
- **Usage:** Shot 2 (wide) for page hero; Shot 3 (both products) for secondary section; Shot 6 (candle close-up) for ambient atmosphere break
- **Conversion intent:** Images support the ask — "Join the waitlist" — without selling. The mood does the convincing.
