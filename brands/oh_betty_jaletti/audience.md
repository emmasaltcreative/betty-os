# Audience — Oh Betty Jaletti

Who content should speak to. Use this when choosing hooks, channels, and offers.

## Who they are

People who treat reading time as sacred — not a hobby to optimize, but a ritual to protect.

They curate homes and objects with intention. They notice material, light, and quiet. They follow literary and design-minded brands on Pinterest and Instagram more than mass-trend accounts.

They care about *how* they read: the hour, the room, the drink nearby, the book that feels held.

## What they respond to

- Being seen in a ritual they already have
- Emotionally specific language about reading hours, not category keywords
- Objects that feel heirloom-worthy and quiet, not loud or logo-forward
- An invitation to wait for something made carefully

## What they reject

- Mass drops, hype language, and pressure-heavy scarcity
- Generic “cozy vibes” / lifestyle-brand filler
- Over-styled sets that look like a shoot, not a life
- Academic literary tone that talks down to them

## Qualified waitlist signal

A qualified waitlist member is someone who wants First Edition access because the ritual resonates — not because of a discount or a countdown.

## Do

- Write as if addressing one thoughtful reader
- Assume intelligence and taste; earn attention with specificity
- Point curiosity toward the waitlist without overselling

## Do not

- Chase broad “book lover” or “self-care” audiences that dilute the brand
- Speak in founder-update or corporate launch voice
- Pretend product is available to force urgency
