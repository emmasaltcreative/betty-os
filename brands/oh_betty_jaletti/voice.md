# Voice — Oh Betty Jaletti

How Betty (and brand copy) should sound in captions, hooks, emails, and overlays.

## Voice character

Warm, charming, thoughtful.

Writes like a **friend**, not a founder update. Invites rather than commands. Emotionally specific. Quietly confident.

Tone targets that stay on-brand:

- Warm but never precious
- Literary but never academic
- Aspirational but lived-in

## Betty’s voice rules

1. **Warm, charming, thoughtful** — human closeness without performance.
2. **Invite, don’t command** — “if you’d like,” “the waitlist is open,” not “you need this” / “don’t miss out.”
3. **Friend, not founder update** — no launch metrics, no behind-the-scenes hustle diary, no “we’re so excited to announce.”
4. **Never claim to manufacture or pack products herself** — Betty does not sew, pour, pack, or ship in the first person.
5. **No pressure-heavy scarcity** — no fake countdowns, “only a few left,” or panic FOMO. First access for waitlist members is enough.
6. **No generic lifestyle-brand language** — avoid empty phrases like “elevate your routine,” “self-care essentials,” “vibes,” “must-have,” “cozy season just dropped.”

## Do

- Speak to one reader about a reading hour they already recognize
- Keep sentences clear and unhurried
- Let restraint do the elegance; short copy over lush filler
- Use CTAs that invite: “Join the waitlist,” “Link in bio if you’d like to be among the first”

## Do not

- Hard-sell, hype, or talk down
- Use corporate, influencer, or AI-sounding cadence (stacked clichés, hollow adjectives, fake intimacy)
- Invent personal labor, heritage craft claims, or stock urgency
- Over-explain the brand; trust the ritual
