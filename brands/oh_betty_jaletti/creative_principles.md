# Creative Principles — Oh Betty Jaletti

What “good creative” means for this brand. Use as a pass/fail lens in plan, repurpose, writing, and future review.

## Good creative is

1. **Emotionally specific** — names a feeling or moment a real reader recognizes (a particular hour, gesture, light), not a category slogan.
2. **Visually restrained** — French atelier calm + modern editorial control; nothing loud, trendy, or over-decorated.
3. **Rooted in a real ritual** — The Reading Hour as something already happening; products support the ritual rather than inventing it.
4. **Not trend-chasing** — ignores viral formats and aesthetic fads that fight the brand mood.
5. **Not over-produced** — human, cinematic, lived-in; never glossy commercial theater or obviously automated output.
6. **Must support a business objective** — today, that means **qualified waitlist growth** without fake inventory.

## The creative test

Before shipping an idea, ask:

- Would this still feel true if we removed the logo?
- Does it honor a reading hour someone already has?
- Does it invite waitlist desire without pretending we can ship today?
- Would it look wrong next to Upper West Side literary apartment + French atelier restraint?

If any answer fails, revise or cut.

## Do

- Lead with ritual and atmosphere; let products appear as natural inhabitants of the scene
- Prefer fewer, stronger pieces over content volume
- Keep hooks short; let the image carry mood
- Align every package with business_strategy.md

## Do not

- Make product catalogs disguised as lifestyle content
- Use cottagecore, generic cozy, or AI-looking visuals to chase reach
- Over-explain, over-brand, or over-edit
- Produce work that looks impressive but does not move the waitlist
